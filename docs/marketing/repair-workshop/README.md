## 2. Six-week curriculum (draft — edit to what you'll really teach)

| Week | Theme | Students will |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations & safety | Electrical and gas safety, tools and multimeter use, reading wiring diagrams and model/serial plates, diagnostic method |
| 2 | Refrigeration | How a fridge cools, sealed system basics, defrost systems, door seals, fans, thermostats, control boards |
| 3 | Washers & dryers | Drive systems, pumps, bearings, belts, heating elements, vents, error codes |
| 4 | Cooking appliances | Ranges, ovens, cooktops: igniters, elements, thermostats, gas valves and safety |
| 5 | Dishwashers & small jobs | Inlet valves, pumps, spray arms, door latches; leaks; microwaves (safety only) |
| 6 | Real-world service | Live diagnosis on mystery units, quoting, parts sourcing, customer communication, starting a repair side business |

Note on the week 2 line: sealed-system work on refrigerators involves refrigerant handling that requires a licence in Canada. Teach it as "understand" unless the instructors hold the right ODS/refrigerant credentials, and don't advertise "refrigerant repair" otherwise.

**Open house idea:** a free 45-minute "Open the Machine" evening where an instructor opens a washer live and answers questions. Easy to fill, builds trust, and converts attendees into sign-ups. Run it 10 days before the start.

**Metrics:** cost per sign-up, landing-page conversion, open-house to enrolment rate, seats filled by source. Set a target such as "50% of seats by T-2 weeks" and cut spend to the best channel if it's missed.

**After cohort 1:** collect real testimonials and before/after photos, then replace all `[Real quote]` slots. Testimonials are the strongest content you'll have, and they can't be made up in advance.
